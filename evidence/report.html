Actuator Lab

One-axis reaction-wheel dynamics, controller comparison and independently checked numerical evidence.

Observed outcome: cascaded current control passed 64/64 frozen trials; direct voltage feedback passed 0/64. The post-observation feedforward ablation passed 10/64 on those unchanged inputs. All use the same outer gains and initial conditions. These synthetic parameters are educational assumptions, with no manufacturer fit or physical validation.

What the experiment measures

The C++20 plant evolves body angle, absolute body and wheel speeds, and motor current. Back EMF and bearing loss depend on relative speed. One SI motor constant enforces torque/back-EMF consistency. Five work/impulse ledgers share the same fourth-order Runge–Kutta stages. Terminal success means the entire last two seconds has |angle error| ≤ 0.01 rad and |body rate| ≤ 0.002 rad/s, with no trip, observed hard-limit excursion or abort.

The cascade compensates nominal back EMF and adds current feedback with conditional integration. The baseline has no back-EMF/current compensation and is not claimed optimally tuned. Its missing back-EMF term forces a steady angle offset when the wheel has stored momentum; a nominal 0.01 N m s pulse predicts the observed 0.105 rad offset. The follow-up feedforward controller keeps the cascade’s current-demand clamp and nominal compensation but removes proportional/integral current feedback (Kc=KI=0). It was added after observing the original results; no inputs were resampled and no gains retuned. Original paired source/results are preserved in checkpoints/original-paired. The soft speed gate cannot unload total angular momentum; sustained external torque ultimately defeats attitude regulation. Zero commanded voltage models closed motor terminals, not an open circuit. A stuck driver can override the safety command.

Verification

121 executed numerical/experiment gates passed. Every C++ experiment was replayed with identical raw trace and summary bytes. Twenty-one named trajectories were separately integrated with SciPy DOP853 in total-momentum/relative-speed coordinates; maximum scaled physical-state difference was 1.47e-06. Independent closed-loop commands were also compared for nominal and pulse cases. The smooth h = 0.0005 s error was 1.54e-08; three step sizes show fourth-order state/work-balance convergence. Tightening the reference tolerance passed its sensitivity check.

Checks cover analytical electrical, friction and external-torque limits, momentum/energy identities, event boundaries, trips/faults, strict CLI admission, failure publication and failed-trial retention. ASan/UBSan and unit/CLI results are recorded separately in final-checks.txt. Maxima are observed at RK substep endpoints; this is not a continuous-time safety proof.
